Interim safety analysis – PANCREATITIS-RCT

Author

Farkas Nelli, Márton Kiss MD

Executive summary

Data cut: 46 patients (10 Jun 2025). Randomised (mITT): insuline and heparin: 15 • no additional treatment: 14 • plasmapheresis: 17 As-treated exposure: insuline and heparin: 13 • no additional treatment: 15 • plasmapheresis: 18

Eleven prespecified safety signals were screened with two-sided Fisher’s exact tests (α = 0.05, no multiplicity adjustment required for safety monitoring). Smallest unadjusted p: 0.030

Two tests reached the 5 % level, signalling inhomogeneity by the mITT definition:

  • Tachycardia (pairwise test confirms the difference between plasmapheresis vs. insulin groups, as in insulin group had more frequent occurrence compared to the plasmapheresis group)
  • Plasmapheresis-related complications (pairwise test confirms the difference between plasmapheresis vs. insulin groups, as in plasmapheresis group had more frequent occurrence compared to the plasmapheresis group)

Statistics alone cannot determine safety. Investigators and the DSMB must weigh these results together with clinical judgement, seriousness of events, biological plausibility, and all other accrued evidence.

The review of the individual patient data is recommended to investigate the clinical relevance of the apparent differences concerning the two AEs mentioned above.

To note, this analysis was made only after the first 46 patients across three groups, and as such, only very marked differences might have been detected. While not planned in the Protocol at this time, but further safety analyses may yield better results in the future, as more patients are enrolled and more data are collected; a repeat analysis after ~50% may be considered.
